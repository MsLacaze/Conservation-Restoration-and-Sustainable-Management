Year 10 Science · Freshwater ecosystems · Team investigation

OPERATION:
SAVE THE STREAM

A freshwater ecosystem is collapsing. You have been recruited as an environmental response team. Investigate the evidence, research real New Zealand solutions, make difficult decisions and unlock the final restoration plan.

Suggested time: 55–70 minutes

🚨 Emergency briefing: Te Awa Kōwhai

Te Awa Kōwhai is a fictional lowland stream in Aotearoa. It flows through farmland before reaching a wetland and joining a larger river. Over the past few years, the stream has become cloudy. Algae cover some slow-flowing sections, fewer aquatic insects are being found, and local people have reported fewer native fish.

Your mission: identify the causes, investigate the science, choose the most effective restoration actions and defend your decisions. You are not trying to guess what the teacher wants. You must use evidence.

Team roles
Researcher · Evidence recorder · Ecologist · Strategy lead. Share the work; everyone must understand the decisions.
Device rules
Use your device to research. Use reliable sources such as DOC, NIWA / Earth Sciences NZ, Ministry for the Environment, councils and universities.
Evidence rule
For each room, record at least one useful source title or URL and explain what evidence you used. Copying a sentence is not an explanation.

How to play: Complete each investigation, enter the room code and submit your evidence notes. A correct code alone is not enough: you must also provide a research note to unlock the next room.

Follow the pollution: Trace the chain reaction

A stream problem is rarely caused by one thing.

Water tests downstream show elevated nitrate and phosphate levels. A week later, a large algal bloom develops. Soon after, dissolved oxygen drops and fish are found gasping near the surface.

Investigation 2A — Reconstruct the sequence

The catchment dilemma: Make the hard decisions

A successful plan must work for the whole catchment, not just one patch.

The catchment includes farms, a small town, a wetland and a stream used for recreation and mahinga kai. Farmers need water for production. Residents want clean water. Native species need healthy habitats. The council has funding for only three major actions this year.

Investigation 4A — Sort the management approaches

Classify each action as Conservation (protect what is still healthy), Restoration (repair what has been damaged), or Sustainable management (manage use to reduce ongoing impacts).

ActionYour classification
Protect an unpolluted spring from development.
Replant a cleared stream margin with suitable native vegetation.
Use water meters and manage irrigation takes to protect minimum flows.

Final room: Guardians of the stream

The final decision is not just scientific. It is about responsibility.

Your team has been invited to present its plan to a community freshwater council. The council wants to know how your decisions reflect kaitiakitanga as well as ecological science.

Final investigation — The guardianship challenge

Research kaitiakitanga in relation to freshwater. Consider wai as taonga, mauri, reciprocal responsibility and the role of mātauranga Māori alongside scientific monitoring. Explain how these ideas could influence real decisions.
